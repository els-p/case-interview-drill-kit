# 03 · Crypto Revenue Down, Market Up

**Type:** cannibalisation · **Time:** 35 min · **Main skill:** who moved, and were they ever at risk?

## Opening prompt (read aloud)

> "Acme's retail crypto trading revenue in the UK and Europe fell about 30% quarter on quarter, from £40M to £28M. Over the same period Bitcoin rose about 20% and industry retail volumes rose about 15%. What's going on, and what would you do?"

<details>
<summary><b>Interviewer only: data key</b> (don't open if you're the candidate)</summary>

Reveal each item only when asked.

**Monetisation**
- Main Acme app: trading fee/spread ~1.45% blended (lower on paid plans).
- Acme Pro Exchange (a separate low-fee venue for active traders): 0.09% in Q3 (0% maker-fee promo; ~0.25% list price).
- Staking commission ~£0.6M a quarter (flat). Liquidity and custody costs are small, not the issue.

**Headline**

| | Q2 | Q3 |
|---|---|---|
| Total crypto volume | £3.0B | £3.3B (+10%) |
| App volume | £2.7B | £1.8B |
| Pro Exchange volume | £0.3B | £1.5B |
| Revenue | £40.0M | £28.0M (−30%) |
| Blended take rate | 1.33% | 0.85% |
| Active traders | 1.20M | 1.15M |

**Segments**
- Top 5% of traders (~60k users) = 70% of app volume in Q2 (£1.89B). In Q3, 80% of them trade mainly on the Pro Exchange. Their total volume grew (£2.19B → £2.4B) but moved from 1.45% to 0.09%.
- Everyone else: app volume £0.81B → £0.90B (+11%, in line with the market).
- New crypto traders 120k → 90k (new UK first-time-investor rules). Small revenue effect (~£0.4M). **Red herring** if over-prioritised.

**What changed**
- In July an in-app campaign pushed the Pro Exchange with 0% maker fees to **all** crypto users.
- No change to main app pricing.

**Was it worth it?**
- Purpose: stop active traders leaving for external exchanges.
- Top traders' withdrawals to external exchanges: £150M → £60M a quarter. ~£90M of leakage stopped, worth only ~£1.3M a quarter at app fees.
- **65% of the traders who moved had never withdrawn to an external exchange.** No sign they were leaving.

**Bridge**
- Rest-of-base growth: +£0.09B × 1.45% = **+£1.3M**
- Top-trader move: −£0.99B × 1.45% = −£14.4M; +£1.2B × 0.09% = +£1.1M → **−£13.3M**
- £40.0M + 1.3 − 13.3 = £28.0M ✓
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Model answer</b></summary>

- **Root cause:** our own Pro Exchange promo moved our most valuable traders from 1.45% to 0.09% (−£13M a quarter). It saved only ~£1M a quarter of leakage, and 65% of the movers were never at risk.
- **Quick fixes:** stop promoting the Pro Exchange to everyone; target only users with leaving signals (external withdrawals, very high volume); end the 0% promo; add volume tiers inside the main app so mid-high traders get a modest discount (e.g. 0.8%) instead of 0.09%. Recovers ~£5–6M a quarter.
- **Long-term fixes:** a deliberate pricing ladder (app → app tiers → Pro Exchange) with eligibility rules; bundle lower crypto fees into a paid subscription; track cannibalisation as a launch metric; track external withdrawals as the real retention measure.
- **Summary:** "Revenue fell 30% while the market grew, because our own promo moved our best traders to a 0.09% venue, costing £13M a quarter to save £1M of leakage. 65% of them were never at risk. I'd retarget the Pro Exchange to traders who show signs of leaving, end the 0% promo and add app volume tiers, recovering about £5–6M a quarter."
- **Common mistake:** keeping the promo without converting its volume into revenue first.
</details>
