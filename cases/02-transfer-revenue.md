# 02 · Transfer Revenue Down, Volume Up

**Type:** pricing / P&L · **Time:** 35 min · **Main skill:** build a revenue bridge

## Opening prompt (read aloud)

> "Acme's international transfer revenue in Singapore fell about 25% quarter on quarter, while transfer volume grew about 15%. What's going on, and what would you do?"

<details>
<summary><b>Interviewer only: data key</b> (don't open if you're the candidate)</summary>

Reveal each item only when asked.

**Monetisation**
- Revenue = FX markup of 0.6% on the converted amount (when charged) + a flat S$3 fee per transfer.
- Standard plan: no markup up to a monthly fee-free FX allowance; 0.6% above it.
- Costs: payout partner S$1.20 per transfer; FX liquidity ~0.05% of volume.

**Headline**

| | Q2 | Q3 |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | S$1.00B | S$1.15B (+15%) |
| Transfers | 500k | 640k (+28%) |
| Average ticket | S$2,000 | ~S$1,800 |
| Revenue | S$6.0M | S$4.51M (−25%) |
| Take rate | 0.60% | 0.39% |
| Contribution (revenue − payout − liquidity) | S$4.9M | S$3.17M (−35%) |

**Revenue split**
- Markup: S$4.5M → S$3.31M. Share of volume charged markup fell **75% → 48%**.
- Fees: S$1.5M → S$1.2M. Fee-paying transfers 500k → 400k.

**What changed (both on 1 July)**
- The Standard plan's fee-free FX allowance was raised from **S$1,250 to S$5,000 a month** to match competitors.
- A "send home free" campaign waived the fee to two corridors (240k fee-free transfers in Q3), marketed to migrant workers.

**Segments**
- +140k transfers mostly from ~90k new senders on those corridors, average ticket ~S$650, almost all fee-free and within the allowance: ~S$0 revenue, ~S$1.53 cost each.
- ~70% of the lost markup comes from **existing** Standard customers sending the same amounts as before. No extra volume from them.
- Existing-sender churn: 2.1% a month before July, 2.0% after. There was no churn problem to fix.
- Competitors charge ~0.5–0.6% all-in.
- 25% of new senders also activated the card; card revenue ~S$1.5 a month each.

**Bridge**
- At Q2 pricing, Q3 volume would earn S$7.1M: **+S$1.1M from growth**.
- Allowance change: **−S$1.87M**.
- Fee waiver: **−S$0.72M**.
- 6.0 + 1.1 − 1.87 − 0.72 = 4.51 ✓
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Model answer</b></summary>

- **Root cause:** July pricing gave away S$1.9M a quarter of markup to existing customers who weren't leaving, plus S$0.7M of fee waivers.
- **Quick fixes:** trim the Standard allowance to ~S$2,500 (recovers ~S$1M a quarter, mostly from existing heavy senders); keep the bigger allowance as a paid-plan benefit; keep the corridor campaign for new users but time-box it or add a minimum fee on small transfers.
- **Long-term fixes:** price by segment and corridor with margin guardrails; direct local payout rails to cut the S$1.20 payout cost; cross-sell the card to remitters; test price sensitivity before company-wide pricing moves.
- **Summary:** "Revenue fell 25% despite 15% more volume, because July's pricing gave S$1.9M a quarter of markup to customers who weren't leaving, plus S$0.7M in fee waivers. I'd trim the allowance and move the large one into paid plans, and keep the remittance growth but make each transfer profitable."
- **Common mistake:** opening with a data question before a frame, goal and tree.
</details>
