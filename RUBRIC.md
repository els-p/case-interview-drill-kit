# Scoring Rubric

Score each dimension from 1 (weak) to 4 (bar for a strong hire). Total out of 32. In practice, **20+ is solid and 24+ is strong**.

| Dimension | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---|
| **Clarify** | Pins down what, where, who, how the product makes money and what changed, in under 4 minutes. States assumptions out loud. |
| **Structure** | Says the tree out loud before asking for data. Breaks the change down in money, and notices when it doesn't add up. |
| **Customers & segments** | Splits by segment (plan, new vs existing, heavy vs light, merchant, device) to find who drives the change. |
| **Solutions** | One quick fix and one long-term fix, each sized in money. The long-term fix changes the system. |
| **Root-cause rigour** | Hypothesis-led, each hypothesis tested with one targeted data request. Checks both revenue and cost branches. |
| **Goals & P&L** | Goal with a money target and deadline. Correct maths, right units, impact in money. |
| **Trade-offs** | Names the user, technical and commercial downsides without being asked. Proposes a test or phased rollout. |
| **Communication** | Signposts, stays on time, ends with a clear 30-second summary: problem → cause → fix → impact. |

## Extra checks for sparse-data cases

- Built profit per customer per plan **without being asked**?
- Stated assumptions out loud, and tested them against the facts?
- Clarified the ambiguous number instead of guessing?

## Common ways to lose points

- Asking for data before giving a frame, goal and tree.
- Building on made-up numbers instead of asking.
- Solving after the first finding without checking the other branch.
- Killing a promotion without checking what its customers are worth.
- A summary that recalculates, adds new ideas, or raises doubts.
