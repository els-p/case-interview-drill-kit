# 60-Second Summary Drills

Each drill gives you the facts and the recommendation. Your job is only to say it in the order **problem → cause → fix → impact**, in 60 seconds, with no new ideas and no recalculating.

## 1. Food delivery

- Monthly order profit fell 2M (−25%) while orders were flat.
- A free-delivery promo for all users launched last month, costing 1.5M a month.
- 70% of promo users were existing customers who already ordered regularly.
- Rider cost per order rose 0.50 because of the rainy season, adding 0.5M.
- Recommendation: limit free delivery to new and lapsed (60+ days) customers. Saves about 1M a month. Rider costs should ease after the season.

<details><summary>Model summary</summary>

"Order profit fell 2M, or 25%, while orders stayed flat. Most of that is the free-delivery promo, which cost 1.5M, and 70% of it went to customers who'd have ordered anyway. Rider costs added 0.5M because of the rainy season, which will ease. I'd limit free delivery to new and lapsed customers. That saves about 1M a month, and with rider costs easing we recover about 75% of the drop."
</details>

## 2. Savings accounts

- Interest margin on savings fell 3M a quarter (−30%) while balances grew 20%.
- A promo offers new savers 4% for 3 months instead of the standard 2.5%. Its extra cost is 2.5M a quarter.
- Central bank rate cuts reduced what we earn on deposits by 0.5M a quarter.
- 60% of promo savers withdraw when the promo ends.
- Recommendation: offer the 4% rate only to savers who have their salary paid in. Saves about 1.5M a quarter. Rate cuts are outside our control.

<details><summary>Model summary</summary>

"Savings margin fell 3M, or 30%, while balances grew 20%. 2.5M of that is the 4% new-saver promo, and 60% of those savers leave once it ends. The other 0.5M is central bank rate cuts, which we can't control. I'd limit the 4% rate to savers who have their salary paid in, since they're likely to stay. That saves about 1.5M a quarter and recovers half the drop."
</details>

## 3. Airline route

- Route profit fell 4M a quarter (−20%) while passengers grew 15%.
- Fares were cut 15% to match a new low-cost rival, reducing profit by 3M a quarter.
- Higher fuel costs reduced profit by 1M a quarter.
- 75% of passengers on the cheaper fares are existing customers who'd have flown anyway.
- Recommendation: keep low fares only on off-peak flights where the rival competes; go back to normal fares on peak flights that sell out. Saves about 2M a quarter. Fuel is outside our control.

<details><summary>Model summary</summary>

"Route profit fell 4M, or 20%, even though passengers grew 15%, so profit per passenger fell about 30%. 3M of that is the 15% fare cut to match the new rival, and 75% of those cheaper fares went to customers who'd have flown with us anyway. The other 1M is fuel, which we can't control. I'd keep low fares only on off-peak flights, where we compete with the rival, and go back to normal fares on peak flights that sell out anyway. That saves about 2M a quarter, half the drop."

Watch out: don't add a second fix that comes out of the same 3M. Two savings from one cost line can't add up to more than the line.
</details>

## 4. International transfers

- Transfer revenue fell 1.2M a month (−20%) while transfer volume grew 10%.
- A fee-free campaign on one corridor reduced revenue by 0.9M a month.
- 80% of the fee-free transfers came from customers who already sent money on that corridor every month.
- A weaker home currency cut FX margin by 0.3M a month. That's outside our control.
- Recommendation: make the fee-free offer apply only to each customer's first 3 transfers. Saves about 0.7M a month.

<details><summary>Model summary</summary>

"Transfer revenue fell 1.2M, or 20%, while volume grew 10%, so revenue per transfer fell about 27%. 0.9M of that is the fee-free campaign, and 80% of it went to customers who were already sending money with us every month. The other 0.3M is the weaker currency, which we can't control. I'd limit fee-free transfers to each customer's first 3. That saves about 0.7M a month, close to 60% of the drop."
</details>
