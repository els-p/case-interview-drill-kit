# The Method

## The 8 steps (35 minutes)

| Time | Step | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | **Silent plan** | Decide your structure and the data you'll need. Don't solve yet. Say: *"Let me take 30 seconds to plan."* |
| ~4 min | **1. Frame** | What's happening, where, who's affected, **how the product makes money**, and what changed recently. |
| ~2 min | **2. Goal** | A target in money with a deadline and a reason. If you don't have numbers yet, use %. |
| ~1 min | **3. Tree** | Say the drivers out loud: number of customers, mix, price, discounts, cost per customer. |
| ~5 min | **4. Break the change down in money** | One line per driver. **It must add up to the total.** If it doesn't, something is missing. |
| ~6 min | **5. Hypotheses** | Find the surprising lines → ask what changed → link the surprises → *"I think X because Y; I'd check it with Z."* Check **both** branches (revenue and cost). |
| ~3 min | **6. What's a customer worth?** | Profit per period × how long they stay, vs cost to acquire. Payback = acquisition cost ÷ profit per period. |
| ~7 min | **7. Fixes** | One quick fix, one long-term fix (a change to the system, not "analyse more"). Size each one. Trade-offs, then a test or phased rollout. |
| 0:30 | **8. Summary** | Problem → cause → fix → impact. **Read it out from your notes; don't work it out.** |

## Habits that move the score

- **Ask before you assume.** If you must assume, say it out loud and say what it's based on.
- **Clarify unclear numbers on the spot.** *"Is that 10 a per-user cost or a fixed monthly cost?"*
- **"No change in process" isn't "no change in losses."** Don't strike off a branch until you've seen its number.
- **Interviewers won't confirm you're right.** Don't fish for "correct". Keep driving.
- **"Let me check where I am"** is fine to say when you're stuck. Look at your 8 steps.
- **Treat stated constraints as fixed.** If they say "we can't roll this back", don't lead with a rollback.

## Maths habits

- **Say the formula before the numbers.** Most slips disappear.
- **Calculate only the change.** For a cost rise: change per customer × customers this period. One multiplication instead of four.
- **Divide, don't add, for per-unit changes.** Profit −20% and customers +10% → 0.8 ÷ 1.1 ≈ 0.73, so profit per customer is −27% (not −30%).
- **Watch your base.** Getting from 0.73 back to 1.0 needs +38%, not +27%.
- **Percentage points vs percent.** 20% → 25% is +5pp, or +25%.
- **Check units.** Monthly vs yearly, revenue vs profit, per customer vs total.
- **Getting back to the old total with more customers** needs less per customer: old total ÷ new customer count.

## When the data is sparse

Some interviewers give almost no absolute numbers: a few unit prices, a mix in %, and "don't know" to some questions. Then:

1. **Build profit per customer for each plan or segment** from the unit numbers (revenue per unit − cost per unit, minus anything free).
2. **Find the break-even:** how much usage a customer needs before they're profitable.
3. **Assume 100 customers** and put illustrative numbers on each segment. Say they're assumptions.
4. **Test assumptions against the facts.** *"If this were a per-user cost, profit would already have been negative last month, so it's probably fixed."*
5. **Split heavy vs light users.** A perk like "unlimited" can turn your most profitable customers into your biggest losses.
6. **Check which team's P&L** gets the revenue and which carries the cost.

## The 30-second summary

> "[Metric] fell [amount, %] even though [the good news]. [Main cause, in money], because [the reason fact]. [Second cause], which [is seasonal / outside our control / fixable]. I recommend [quick fix] and [long-term fix]. That recovers about [amount], [share] of the drop, within [time], while [what we protect]."

No new ideas, no recalculating, no doubts in the summary.

## Questions to ask at the end

- How does this team measure success?
- What's the biggest product bet this year?
- What separates great PMs here?
